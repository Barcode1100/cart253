 ## Prototype Instructions

This was my first real time coding in javascript and i felt like i learned so much. When working on the first prototype (Rainbow), I wanted to learn the coordinates and how sizing and shaping for shapes worked. I struggled aligning the different circles evenly and figuring out what values the x and y points needed to be. In the end though i felt way more confident in trying different and more complex shapes. A simple rainbow was a good start for me as it allowed me to figure out how colours worked in p5 as well! 

What was harder was the graphing of the triangle in my next prototype "House (no MD)". This prototype was meant to be another simpler one, one to get my used to how different shapes worked. In the end i landed with a simple large square with a triangle on top, a rectangle for a door and two parallel window squares. When looking at it i realised, "this is a little too boring". I wanted to see what i could turn this house into. I went on p5 to see if i could expand what this house could possibly be. Now it moves. Slowly. I first had each individual shape rotate at different speeds, eventually all falling together to recreate the house for a split second. I liked this idea but i found it to be an eyesore and i couldnt look at it for more than 30 seconds. I then thought it was so funny to make everything move incredibly slowly. The walls of the house moving one way and the rest of the shapes moving another. I had the values set so low it took 2 minutes to see a slight change in the house and for the sake of respecting everyones time, i increased the values to what we have now. I am incredibly proud of this prototype as i pushed the boundaries of what i thought i could do. 

For my last prototype, i wanted something more "abstract" so i delved into the realms of horror and wanted to see how scary i could make something out of simple p5 shapes. What i got was a rudementary creature made out of circles and ovals. Great. it was not scary and i realised with my current skills, maybe i was better off trying to go for something "off putting". I looked at the references and decided to use the random function to make every shape tremble. While not scary, opening that project up and truly staring at it for a while, it starts to become very creepy. Its sitting there...trembling... as if it was trying to move but it was bound in place.
